Thank you for considering my application to be USAFA's next Permanent Professor of Astronautics. I have spent a career building hands-on engineering skills, cultivating a passion for education in myself and others, and preparing leaders for the future. Together, I believe these skills and experiences make me uniquely qualified for this position. 

I have taught engineering at USAFA for 5 years, covering a range of foundational and advanced classes in both the Astronautical and Mechanical Engineering Departments. During my second assignment, I have been able to significantly update the curricula for two courses to keep up with advances in engineering and ensure our graduates are prepared to work in a modern engineering environment. 

In addition to classroom instruction, I also served as Astro's assessments officer, tracking accreditation metrics and performing annual program reviews to ensure our Astronautical Engineering program lives up to its world-leading reputation. In this role I also led the Engineering Division's biennial Engineering Program Advisory Council to ensure that all of USAFA's engineering graduates are ready to lead in the technical leadership roles they will fill upon graduation. The division's reviewers consistently highlighted a weakness in our technical communication instruction. To address this shortfall, I developed a multi-department course combining foundational engineering skills and technical communication. The course appears to be on track for Fall 2026 inclusion in USAFA's COI as a requirement for two academic majors. 

My favorite part of classroom instruction at USAFA has always been the opportunity it affords for inspiring cadets to care about developing their leadership skills. This spring I was able to greatly expand this part of my job as Acting Commander for USAFA Cadet Squadrons 32 and 37 while their commanders were on extended leave. It was an honor to be directly entrusted with the professional development of 100 cadets. Together with two NCOs, we prepared cadets for successful completion of the inaugural CULEX while navigating a dynamic leadership environment and daily maintaining good order and discipline in the squadron. 

This was not my first time directly supporting the vital role of commissioning education. While in grad school, I helped keep NC State's AFROTC Det 595 afloat for a semester after the commander was relieved of duty. I coordinated coverage from AF graduate students to fill three instructor positions, and I personally taught the sophomore ROTC class at NC State (AFROTC Det 595) on for a semester. This class on Team and Leadership Fundamentals prepared 27 cadets for field training immediately after completing the class. 

My most challenging leadership experience was as branch chief for a team of acquisition intelligence analysts at DIA's Office of Defense Technology and Long-Range Analysis in Charlottesville VA. I had to rebuild the branch after 75% turnover when the office returned to work after a 9-month hiatus for COVID. In the process, I also reversed a years-long decline in office culture that saw enormous distrust develop between military and civilian analysts. This work was only possible due to a team of awesome domain leads. I invested heavily in developing their leadership skills and led two of them to Agency-level annual awards. 

I am proud to be part of USAFA's rich heritage of space education, which includes establishing the nation's first undergraduate degree in Astronautical Engineering and an absolute commitment to teaching a core space class to all graduates. This commitment has prepared countless officers—including me—to succeed when they unexpectedly found themselves in space-focused jobs vital to national defense. 

I believe I have the skills, experience, and passion to succeed as USAFA's next Permanent Professor of Astronautics, and to guide the institution's growing relationship with a nascent Space Force. I hope you agree, and I look forward to the opportunity to discuss with you.
